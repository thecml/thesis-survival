\begin{frame}{Answers to the research questions}
\footnotesize
\begin{columns}[T]
  \begin{column}{0.48\textwidth}
    \rqhead{RQ1 -- Uncertainty}
    MC dropout and SNGP are cheaper alternatives to VI: MC dropout matches VI on MIMIC-IV (0.748 vs.\ 0.743) with half the parameters; SNGP is D-calibrated on 4/4 datasets (VI: 2/4).\par\vspace{0.25em}
    \limit efficient uncertainty does not by itself make predictions trustworthy; conformal coverage holds on average, not per subgroup (74\% for early-stage TMJ).
  \end{column}
  \begin{column}{0.48\textwidth}
    \rqhead{RQ2 -- Multiple events}
    MENSA predicts competing or co-occurring events in one network: competitive with strong baselines, with fewer parameters than one model per event. In ALS, five curves give a fuller picture than one event time.\par\vspace{0.25em}
    \limit the gains from joint training and the trajectory loss are small relative to run-to-run variability.
  \end{column}
\end{columns}
\vspace{1.2em}
\begin{columns}[T]
  \begin{column}{0.48\textwidth}
    \rqhead{RQ3 -- Evaluation}
    Match the metric to the objective and state the censoring assumption -- about 72\% of 92 surveyed papers do not. IBS-Dep cuts IBS estimation error by 12--16\% vs.\ KM-based IPCW.\par\vspace{0.25em}
    \limit lower estimation error did not help pick the best models, and IBS-Dep still assumes a copula for $E$ and $C$.
  \end{column}
  \begin{column}{0.48\textwidth}
    \rqhead{RQ4 -- Beyond mortality}
    Fall risk: 14 points better than chance at ranking (C-index 0.64). Bearings: C-index up to 0.70 (XJTU) and 0.76 (PRONOSTIA).\par\vspace{0.25em}
    \limit fall timing could not be predicted (95\% censoring), alarm subscription is only a proxy for falls, and there were very few physical bearings in the datasets.
  \end{column}
\end{columns}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Future work}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Interpretability}: interpretable deep survival models vs.\ post-hoc explanations (SurvSHAP \cite{Krzyzinski2023SurvSHAPt}, SurvLIME \cite{Kovalev2020SurvLIME}) -- and how to evaluate them.
  \vspace{0.6em}
  \item \textbf{Evaluation}: evaluate event times and probabilities directly; dependence-aware C-index and D-calibration by swapping the KM correction for a copula-based one.
  \vspace{0.6em}
  \item \textbf{Foundation models}: pretrained (MOTOR \cite{steinberg2024motor}) and in-context survival models (SurvivalPFN \cite{qi2026survivalpfn}, SurvPFN \cite{bohm2026survpfn}) -- do they generalize across censoring mechanisms?
\end{itemize}
\end{frame}
